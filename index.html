What is URIM? · Coming soon

A mathematical model of the decision behind the case.

URIM is being developed to represent a dispute as a system of observed facts, unresolved events, possible outcomes, and available actions—then expose the assumptions and dependencies behind the resulting decision.

The intended mathematical object

Not a score. A distribution over possible paths.

URIM is being designed to model the joint uncertainty connecting the current record, unresolved events, terminal outcomes, and decisions available to the user.

Proposed joint predictive object p(Y, Z, θ, M | X, D)

Marginalize over unresolved events, uncertain parameters, and candidate models to calculate the quantity relevant to the decision.

D
Historical comparison data
X
Observed matter record
Z
Unresolved factual, legal, and procedural events
Y
Possible terminal outcomes
A
Actions available to the decision-maker
01

Define the dependency structure.

Represent which unresolved events enable, prevent, or materially change later events and outcomes.

Z₁ → Z₂ → Y
02

Propagate uncertainty.

Compute a predictive distribution rather than hiding uncertainty behind a categorical answer.

q = p(Y = 1 | X, D)
03

Measure scenario influence.

Recompute the output under explicit alternative conditions to identify the events with the greatest leverage.

Δᵢ = q(Zᵢ=1) − q(Zᵢ=0)
04

Compare available actions.

Relate possible outcomes to value, cost, duration, and downside under a disclosed decision rule.

A* = arg max E[U(A,Y) | X,D]
A range needs a definition.

For a binary outcome, p(Y = 1 | X, D) is a scalar. Any interval shown by URIM must say whether it is a posterior credible interval, a frequentist confidence interval, or a distribution across an explicitly weighted scenario set. These are not interchangeable.

Evidence standard

Trust has to be earned in the method.

URIM will not publish a corpus count, win-rate claim, or performance figure until it is real, dated, reproducible, and scoped to the matter class and jurisdiction tested.

  1. 01

    Temporal testing

    Evaluate on decisions the system could not have seen.

  2. 02

    Calibration first

    Test whether estimated probabilities correspond to observed frequencies.

  3. 03

    Failure inventory

    Publish exclusions, abstentions, missing data, and material failure modes.

  4. 04

    Versioned change

    Record changes to data, models, review, and affected briefs.

Data security, privacy, and governance

Professional control stays visible.

Matter minimization

The public site requests only broad portfolio context. It does not accept party names, documents, confidential facts, or privileged strategy.

Human review

The instrument informs professional judgment. Counsel remains responsible for legal analysis, client advice, and every decision taken.

Versioned provenance

The intended brief records its source basis, assumptions, model and data version, analyst review, and reason for revision.
